Keep compact source-based head/body and limb proportions for each character. Never apply a universal ratio to the whole cast. Apparent foreshortening must follow the camera; nonuniform image scaling is prohibited. Original turntable sheets do not establish relative heights between characters.

## Japanese scene comparison

Each style uses the same narrative suite: **A day of reading in a Japanese neighbourhood**.

1. Wide hero: quiet neighbourhood bookshop and tea garden; all six participating in reading, tea, writing, finding a route, examining a leaf and tending a plant. Large clear left area reserved for live headline and CTA.
2. Reading vignette: Koko and Gohanko at a low table with a book and tea, shoji and an engawa garden edge.
3. Discovery vignette: Bear, Rabbit, Yauyau and Tree in a neighbourhood garden path with a map, notebook and potted plants.

Use everyday architecture and meaningful activities. Avoid filling every scene with unrelated Japanese symbols. Props should explain the action. Decorative written Japanese is omitted in concept art; final signage and learning text must be checked and typeset separately.

## Landing-page recommendation and decision

Start with **A — Flat Adventure** as the leading candidate: its simpler fill/line system should accommodate both open hero layouts and smaller supporting illustrations. Compare D if warmth and continuity with the existing 365 library matter most. Choose C if an explorable neighbourhood is central to the page. B is most distinctive for energetic campaigns but requires the most restraint around reading content.

Use one chosen finish throughout the landing page. Other styles can live in clearly separate campaigns, articles or product experiences; do not mix finishes within one scene. Keep the logo, UI typography and interface colours independent of illustration finish.

Judge the scene boards on identity, reading hierarchy, cultural coherence, welcoming tone and ease of reuse. These are visual recommendations, not performance or conversion findings. The actual landing page is not changed by this exploration.

## Production and platform rules

- Every new design must include a layered editable master in addition to flattened preview/export files. Keep background, decorative motifs, live text, logo, each character, held props and foreground elements on named layers. A flattened PNG alone is not a production deliverable.
- Use SVG for vector-led compositions and Figma/Illustrator interchange. Where character artwork remains raster, supply each character and prop as a separate transparent PNG linked from the SVG; record the links in a manifest. Do not embed official logos into generated pixels.
- Maintain layered masters: setting, ground, character, held prop, foreground and live text independently editable.
- Derive wide, square and portrait compositions from the same scene components. Reposition components on mobile instead of cropping faces or uniformly shrinking a busy scene.
- Test isolated poses at 64/96/160 CSS pixels and scene cards at their actual placement sizes before export. These are proposed tests, not passed thresholds.
- Keep essential features visible at intended size. Decorative art uses empty alt text; informative art gets concise alt text conveying its purpose.
- Provide static alternatives to motion. Prepare rigs and Lottie assets separately after the finish and construction sheets are approved; these PNGs do not contain editable animation.
- Name new files by current style and purpose, e.g. A-characters-v1.png and A-japanese-scenes-v1.png. Preserve previous revisions and keep a review log.
